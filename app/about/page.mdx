I studied for a networking degree, majoring in software engineering, at Melbourne Institute of Technology, finishing in 2025. Our capstone, a payment gateway for a real client, won overall at the university's IMPACT 2025 capstone showcase.

Since then I've done four internships. At the Royal Humane Society I digitised handwritten award records going back to the 1870s. Tesseract couldn’t read the handwriting, so the OCR moved to a paid service — Mistral OCR first, then LLMWhisperer — with a daily spend limit checked before each call rather than after. The staff got a search page over 150 years of reports with CSV export. The part that was meant to pull out structured fields ran on a local model on the hardware they had, and it invented values; I handed that over labelled experimental and made the reference data for several decades by hand instead. At Audacix I worked across a web security scanner and chose the local model behind its assistant. At EdgenAI I built LangGraph workflows and output guardrails for a rubric generator. At Silverpond I designed an agent architecture for a multi-tenant platform and built it into their Rails product.

Alongside those I completed a Professional Year program, August 2025 to August 2026 — an Australian graduate program of workplace training and a professional placement. Silverpond was the placement.

Outside work I build Ovela, a voice receptionist, and Agent-OS, a harness for operating a Mac. Both are open source, with their measurements and limits in the README.

## How I think

Learn deeply. Unlearn quickly. Build against reality.

I tend to notice the things that don't fit the expectation, and sometimes I follow them further than I need to. That's also where most of my useful engineering decisions have come from. I like leaving a problem with a reasoned decision — and enough evidence to change my mind later.

Away from a screen: long drives, with the music loud.

## How I use AI assistants

Much of my implementation is written with AI coding assistants, and the commit counts on these pages include that work. The architecture, the measurements, the debugging and the corrections, including the claims I've withdrawn, are mine.
